Learning to roleplay with strangers online is one of the most rewarding ways to grow as a storyteller. A stranger brings ideas you would never think of, reacts in ways you cannot predict, and pushes you to adapt. It can also feel a little intimidating at first. What do you say? What if they are rude? How much should you share? This guide covers the etiquette that makes you a great partner and the safety habits that keep the experience fun.

## Why play with strangers at all?

Friends are great roleplay partners, but they come with history. You know their tastes, they know yours, and stories can settle into familiar grooves. Strangers reset all of that.

- **You get fresh ideas.** Every partner has a different sense of drama, humor, and pacing.
- **You improve faster.** Adapting to new styles is the quickest way to sharpen your own.
- **There is no pressure to impress.** If a scene flops, you both move on.
- **You might find a long-term partner.** Some of the best creative partnerships start with a random match.

On SweetScene, playing with strangers is the whole point. You are matched anonymously with a real person, an AI director sets the scene and voices side characters, and you both improvise. Afterward, you can both choose to reveal profiles and keep talking, or skip to the next match.

## Etiquette: how to be a partner people remember

Good etiquette is mostly about generosity. You are building something together, so leave room for the other person.

### Open with something to react to

Your first message sets the tone. Instead of "hey what do you want to do," start in character with a clear action and an opening.

> *The space station's lights flicker to emergency red. Tamsin hauls herself through the hatch, out of breath, and spots you by the airlock controls.* "Please tell me you know how to override that, because I definitely don't."

Your partner now knows the genre, the stakes, and has a direct question to answer.

### Do not control their character

This is the golden rule. Describe what your character does, not what theirs does or feels. Write "she swings the lantern toward the doorway" rather than "she swings the lantern and hits him." Let your partner decide the outcome. Taking control of another player's character is often called godmodding, and it is the fastest way to lose a partner.

### Match their energy and length

If your partner writes a few lines, a long essay can feel overwhelming. If they write rich paragraphs, a single sentence can feel dismissive. Aim to roughly match. It does not need to be exact, just comfortable.

### Add something every turn

Each reply should move the story forward in some small way: a new action, a question, a reveal, a complication. Replies like "*nods*" or "ok" stall the scene. If you are stuck, have your character notice something in the environment and let the AI director run with it.

### Respect the vibe

If you matched through By Vibe on a specific mood, like Romance with a slow-burn feel or Adventure with exploration, stay within it. That mood is a shared agreement about the kind of story you both wanted.

### Use out-of-character notes sparingly and kindly

Brief notes in parentheses are normal and useful:

- "(want to time-skip to the ball?)"
- "(not really into horror, can we keep it mysterious instead?)"
- "(sorry for the slow reply, back now)"

Keep them short so they do not break the flow, and always be polite.

### End gracefully

If you need to leave, say so. A quick "(have to head out, this was fun!)" is far kinder than vanishing. If the story is reaching a natural end, steer toward a satisfying beat.

## Consent and comfort in shared stories

Roleplay is collaborative, which means both people should be comfortable with where it goes.

- **Check in before big turns.** If you want to introduce something intense, like a betrayal or a character's injury, a quick "(okay if things get darker here?)" is considerate.
- **Accept a no gracefully.** If your partner steers away, follow their lead without arguing.
- **Fade to black when needed.** Even in romantic scenes, you can imply moments rather than describe them. Keep things tasteful.

## Safety: protecting yourself while you play

Creative freedom works best on a foundation of safety. Here is what to keep in mind.

### Keep your identity out of the story

Your character is not you. Avoid putting your real name, city, school, workplace, or other identifying details into your scenes. It is easy to slip, for example, by naming your character's hometown after your own. Make things up instead.

### Notice red flags

Most partners are just there to tell a story. Still, watch for warning signs:

1. Repeated questions about your age, location, or real identity.
2. Pressure to move to another app or share contact details.
3. Attempts to push the story somewhere you have said no to.
4. Guilt-tripping when you want to leave.

If you see any of these, you do not owe anyone an explanation. Skip and report.

### Take your time after a reveal

A mutual reveal means you both enjoyed the scene. It does not mean you have to share anything beyond your SweetScene profile. Keep chatting in DMs for as long as you like before deciding whether to share more, and it is completely fine if you never do.

## Handling awkward moments

Sooner or later, a scene will go sideways. Here is how to handle the common ones.

### Your partner goes quiet

Give them a little time. If nothing happens, a light "(still there?)" is fine. If they have left, skip and move on.

### You disagree about direction

Try steering in character first, for example by having your character suggest a different plan. If that does not work, a short out-of-character note helps: "(I was hoping for more mystery than combat, could we lean that way?)"

### Someone is rude

You do not have to engage. Skip, and if it was serious, report it.

### You made a mistake

Maybe you misread a detail or controlled their character by accident. A quick "(oops, ignore that, your call)" fixes most things.

## FAQ

### Is it safe to roleplay with strangers online?

It can be, especially on platforms with anonymity, mutual reveal, moderation, and age separation. Keep personal details out of your scenes and skip anyone who pressures you.

### What is godmodding?

Godmodding is controlling another player's character or making your own character succeed at everything without giving the other player a say. Avoid it by describing only your own character's actions.

### What should I do if a partner asks for my personal information?

You do not have to share anything. Decline, skip the match, and report it if they keep pushing.

### How do I end a roleplay politely?

Steer toward a satisfying final moment if you can, or leave a short out-of-character note saying you need to go. Simply disappearing is less kind.
